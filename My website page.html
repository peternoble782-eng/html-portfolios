How To Cook Nigerian Jollof Rice

Nigerian Jollof Rice is one of the most popular and delicious dishes in West Africa. It is a rich, smoky and flavourful one-pot rice dish that is perfect for any occasion.

Ingredients
  • 3 cups of long grain parboiled rice
  • 500g tomato paste or blended fresh tomatoes
  • 3 scotch bonnet peppers (ata rodo)
  • 1 large onion
  • 3 cups of chicken or beef stock
  • 1 cup of vegetable oil
  • 2 seasoning cubes (Maggi or Knorr)
  • 1 teaspoon of curry powder
  • 1 teaspoon of thyme
  • Bay leaves (optional)

Instructions

  1. Blend the Pepper Base

    Blend the tomatoes, red bell peppers, scotch bonnet peppers and half of the onion together until smooth. Set aside.

  2. Fry the Tomato Mixture

    Heat the vegetable oil in a large pot over medium heat. Slice the remaining onion and fry until golden. Add the blended pepper mixture and fry for 20 to 30 minutes until the raw smell is gone and the oil floats to the top.

  3. Season the Sauce

    Wash the rice thoroughly and add it to the pot. Stir well to combine with the sauce. The liquid should just cover the rice. Cover the pot tightly with foil paper first then place the lid on top.

  4. Cook the Rice

    Cook on low to medium heat for 30 minutes. Check every 10 minutes and stir gently to prevent burning at the bottom. Add a little water if needed.

  5. Achieve the Party Rice Smoky Flavour

    In the last 5 minutes, turn the heat to high for 2 to 3 minutes to get that famous smoky bottom known as party Jollof Rice. Then immediately turn off the heat.

  6. Serve

    Fluff the rice with a fork and serve hot with fried chicken, moi moi, coleslaw or plantain. Enjoy your meal!

    7. Cooking Tips

    • Always use parboiled rice for best results
    • The foil paper trick keeps the steam in and cooks the rice perfectly
    • Do not add too much water — Jollof rice should not be soggy
    • The smoky flavour at the end is what makes Nigerian Jollof Rice special

    Serving Size

    This recipe serves 4 to 6 people. Double the ingredients for larger gatherings
